Even though its the summer where I'm supposed to have the most free time it has somehow felt too
little. Maybe its because I'm using my time poorly but there is just so much I want to do this
summer and its just not fitting. There are subjects I want to learn, media that I want to consume,
and friends to spend time with.

A part of it is regret that I didn't spend time on some of these things in previous years. Thinking
about the past is in my opinion futile but I do sometimes wonder how many hours I wasted when I was
younger. There's this concept of enjoying one's youth but at the same time I see people that clearly
have achieved this and yet are much more accomplished. This is a musing about time wasted, and its
nature.

## I Might Be Lazy

Even though it seems like I've been doing a lot the about of work I've done is honestly not even
close to what it could be. My sleep schedule has just gotten worse as time as passed making wake up
extremely late with little to no motivation.

But the biggest issue is the endless scroll absolutely sucking away time without me even noticing. I
might talk more about this independently but I've realized that this aspect of social media is might
have the most destructive long term effects on an individual and I'm attempting to ween myself off
of it. My life was much better before YouTube shorts and Instagram reels and I would like to go back
that.

The one thing I won't compromise on is taking a nap in the afternoon if I can because it allows me
to wake up feeling refreshed but right now because of my horrible sleep at night I end up sleeping
for way to long.

## Time is Weird

Our perception of time is really annoying at times. When you're doing something interesting or
important time often just flies past and yet the most excruciating moments feel so stretched out.
This fact of life annoys me to no end because why can't it be the other way around. Why can't the
best times also feel the longer or at least normal? Why does the clock move so quickly when I'm in a
timed test but screech to a halt during lecture?

Of course a neurologist might be able to derive a biological answer but I would like to approach
this question from a different angle. There's an important value I found in having fun moments feel
so short. I think many fun things would lose a lot of joy if we experienced them slower. As such it
is in our best interest to take the fun and condense it so that it doesn't wear away. When we do
important things like tests we are so focused on the goal in front of us that even though time is so
important we counter intuitively care little about it. This idea is simply that we don't actually
care how long it takes because we have to do it no matter the time.

The way we value such things may slightly explain why we have shorter perceptions of time but what
value does longer perceptions add? Its a self imposed punishment seemingly for not doing something
we enjoy or what we don't value. This may make it so that we try not to do these things in the
future to focus on the more "important" things but this isn't the whole story.

> Dunbar loved shooting skeet because he hated every minute of it and the time passed so slowly. \-
> Yossarian [Catch-22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catch-22)

This quote in Catch-22 is about a soldier Dunbar that strives to be as bored and annoyed possible to
make it feel as if he has spent more time on earth compared his fellow soldiers. As a soldier in WW2
his fear of death turns boredom into a powerful tool for extending his perceived life. He hates it
and yet he prefers it over dying and not experiencing anything. Dunbar of all characters live rent
free in my mind because he somewhat reminds me of myself. Whenever I'm bored or after a period of
time that felt way to fast I remember his approach to life. There is times were I yearn to pass time
slowly to see the clock move as slowly as possible.

I find the argument that we need to be able to feel bored in order to really value the those
important and fun moments in our life but I think being bored is entirely unrelated to anything
else. The value of boredom I think allows us to value time itself. We hate it because it puts so
much our focus on time itself. Boredom forces us to think about it instead of anything else and
those long periods of perceived time make us value the time spent on things.

I think is the ultimate value of these perceptions of time is so that we can focus to us matter more
than time itself when needed but also remind us how precious it is in the moments between where
we're doing very little.
